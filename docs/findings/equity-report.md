# MiWay Transfer-Sync Tool — equity report

Who benefits and who loses, by transfer stop. Run on the bundled weekday feed, **All Day** period, **ridership** weighting, solver **OPTIMAL**, 6,000 connections across 272 transfer stops.

> delta_minutes is the weighted change in passenger-facing wait at the stop (negative = riders wait less). riders_at_stop_proxy sums the average-weekday boardings of the routes serving the stop: published APC figures where MiWay publishes them, calibrated estimates (scripts/build_ridership_dataset.py) otherwise -- see docs/ridership-coverage.md for how the estimates are built. A connection is counted worse-off when its effective wait grows. CAVEAT (APC quantization): routes below APC resolution in boardings_routes.csv (2026-09-05 full rebuild: 14, 25, 31, 73, 90; late-night nearly network-wide) have measured floors near zero because sub-bucket loads never cross the sensor's 20% buckets; their ridership.csv entries are supply-based hours priors (hours-fallback), not measured data -- treat stop weights dominated by them as prior-based, not measured. Another instrument only tap-level data replaces.

> **Data currency:** rider volumes are from the MiWay 2024 Report to > the Community (October 2024 board period) plus calibrated estimates > — they may be outdated. Verify against current figures before quoting.

## Equity framing

Every wait-minute saved is concentrated at specific stops — typically the hubs and cross-intersections the optimizer re-phased. The stops that lose are the other side of the same coin: a route shifted to serve one transfer can lengthen waits at another. The table above names those places, so any council discussion can weigh the concentrated wins against the concentrated losses instead of arguing about averages. The riders_at_stop proxy shows how many people each stop's gain or loss actually touches (published APC where available, calibrated estimates elsewhere -- a route with real boarding data should be ground-truthed before weighing it).
